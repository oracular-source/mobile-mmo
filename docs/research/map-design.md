# Map design: worth exploring, never lost

Third design deep dive (Nov 17, 2026). The owner wants Zone 1 reworked from a sandbox into a real place: a graveyard, a rat tunnel, a forest walk to Brother Malrec. The question: what makes a map feel like it's worth wandering, while still having a clear goal and good sightlines so it never "takes you nowhere"? Looked at Disney park design, Breath of the Wild, Elden Ring, Dark Souls, Hollow Knight, A Link to the Past, EverQuest, WoW and OSRS. No game changes.

## The short version
1. **Landmarks you can steer by.** Every area needs one tall, lit or noisy thing (a "weenie") that tells you where you are and pulls you toward it.
2. **Hide, then reveal.** Block the view (tree lines, walls, fog, hills), then open it up as you move, so something new appears every few steps.
3. **Main roads plus little side paths.** A clear road to each area, with small detours that reward the curious.
4. **Loops, not dead ends.** A dead end tells players "done here"; a loop keeps them wondering what else is around the corner. Shortcuts back to the hub save walking.
5. **Easy at the door, hard at the bottom.** Dungeons and caves get tougher the deeper you go, with the named at the end (EverQuest's Blackburrow).
6. **A wandering named teaches respect.** A tough mob that walks around a starting zone becomes everyone's shared story (WoW's Hogger).
7. **Light and paths lead the eye.** Lit exits, worn roads and critters work as breadcrumbs. On a phone you see only about 14 tiles across, so guidance has to work up close.

## What these games teach

### Disney parks: the "weenie"
Disney's designers use landmarks they call weenies (or beckoning hands) to pull guests along; Cinderella's Castle is the most famous one, and it's nearly impossible to get lost in a Disney park because there's always something of interest close by. A GDC talk on Disneyland level design adds that besides the main arteries, there should be lots of little side-paths that make you feel smart, which reward players for taking different routes. Elden Ring's Erdtree works as a weenie the same way.

### Breath of the Wild: the triangle rule
Hyrule is made mostly of triangle-shaped hills and mountains that hide what's behind them; as you walk around a slope, new points of interest slowly come into view. The obstacles also come in different sizes so the landscape keeps catching the eye. Even though players never felt led, almost all of them eventually reached the key locations. **For us (top-down):** the same trick with tree clumps, walls, ruins and fog banks that block part of the screen, then open onto a new view.

### Hide and reveal, breadcrumbs and light
Designers use a "hide and reveal" trick: the landmark is hidden from view now and then and revealed again from a different angle as you go. Lighting can draw attention to exits, points of interest and enemies. One studio made sure players could always see the nearest light to stay oriented, and found that seeing more than one lit marker at once confused them. **For us:** torches at area entrances, the chapel's candles at night, one light at a time along a path.

### Loops and shortcuts (Dark Souls, Zelda)
A looping layout keeps players asking "have I done everything?", while a dead end, real or apparent, marks the edge of the space, so once a player clears it they forget about it. Dark Souls' world branches into different areas and then loops back on itself through shortcuts and elevators. The way it loops back makes finding your way feel natural and lets you reach new areas without fast travel.

### Hollow Knight: maps you earn
You don't get a region's map until you find Cornifer, the wandering cartographer hidden somewhere in that region. Players spend the first hours disoriented, then start paying attention to the world itself, and remembering landmarks starts to matter. **For us:** maybe later, a map that fills in as you explore. Never quest arrows.

### EverQuest: Qeynos Hills and Blackburrow
Qeynos Hills, the zone outside the starting city, mixes rats, gnolls, wolves and skeletons from levels 1 to 4 right where you enter. Next to it sits Blackburrow, a gnoll dungeon that is half underground and half outdoor hunting area. Like a typical dungeon, the easier mobs are near the front and the harder ones deeper inside, and the named Lord Elgnub waits in the Lord's Room. Players remember it "from the top of the great pit" to the Commanders' room. Old players also credit EverQuest's mix of low and rare high-level mobs in the same zone for creating memorable enemies, and recall the apprehension of a zone line that faded into fog. **For us:** the rat tunnel is our Blackburrow: a pit near the road, easy rats at the mouth, nastier ones deeper, the named at the bottom.

### WoW: Hogger and Elwynn Forest
Hogger is an elite gnoll who roams Elwynn Forest. He is one of the first elites a new human character meets, so new players underestimate him, and he became a running joke and legend. At half health three minions spawn. Players also praise how the human starting zones blend into one another while staying distinct, like the canopy growing thicker as you walk into Duskwood. **For us:** Greyfang is our Hogger (he already howls for his pack). The forest should get darker and denser step by step toward the chapel.

### Elden Ring: a start area with four parts
Limgrave splits into a central region, a forest to the east, a hill to the north and the castle dungeon in the northwest. The castle is the clear goal you can see from the start, while the open land in between is full of ruins and caves to build up on. **For us:** the same shape at small scale, with a central crossroads, three areas around it and the dungeon as the visible goal.

### OSRS: pick a direction
When you leave the tutorial island in Old School RuneScape you're dropped outside a castle in Lumbridge and you just pick a direction. Lumbridge was a castle surrounded by forests, a swamp and many farms where players gathered. **For us:** the crossroads should offer three directions that each look inviting from the start.

## Rules for our Zone 1
1. **One theme, one landmark, one named per area.** The landmark is visible (or audible, or lit) from that area's entrance.
2. **Three to five things to find per area:** camps, story props, a small secret (side path, hidden chest, rare spawn).
3. **Every path loops or shortcuts home.** No walk back down a long dead end.
4. **Difficulty grows with distance and depth,** not with locked doors. Wander too far early and it hurts (EverQuest-style danger), but nothing is blocked.
5. **Breadcrumbs that fit a phone:** worn road edges, torches at entrances, critters fleeing ahead of you, fog thinning toward the way out.
6. **Areas blend:** grass turns to dead grass near the graveyard, trees close in toward the chapel, ground turns to dirt and bones near the rat pit.

## Owner decisions (Nov 17)
- **Size:** about 2–3 minutes for a straight walk from the gate to the far end. Recommendation: about **128 × 128 tiles** (today's map is 40 × 30), a winding road of roughly 250 tiles to the chapel (about 2 minutes at the Druid's ~2 tiles a second), and each area's entrance about 30 seconds from the crossroads.
- **Shared, summonable named:** the rat tunnel and the crypt are open, shared areas. At the named's spot, touching an object (bones, a severed paw, the throne) summons it if it isn't up.
- **First instanced dungeon:** Brother Malrec's chapel, not the crypt.
- **Map:** fills in as you explore.
- **Secrets:** a hidden chest here and there that rolls gear for your class. **Rare spawns:** tougher, more XP, a better drop. **Lore:** later, once the setting is decided.
- **The Mire:** stays out of Zone 1 for now.
- **Follow-up (owner):** 2 minutes end to end to start (about 128 × 128 tiles), then judge it on the phone. Summoning needs **only** a key dropped by that area's mobs; the key is used up, no cooldown. Rares are **1 in 100** spawns, and each area's rare has its own name and look (placeholders in `design.md`). Malrec's dungeon: **a crypt under the chapel altar plus cult halls** (rooms below).

- **Malrec's dungeon (owner, Nov 17):** a little shorter to start, since many more dungeons will go big: **4 rooms** (chapel nave, flooded catacomb, cult halls, Malrec's sanctum), about 16 mobs, a **10–15 minute run**. The catacomb's **slowing water stays**. **Malrec is level 11.** Full layout in `design.md` (Dungeon 1).

## Layout pass 2 (Nov 17, sketch: "Mobile MMO — Zone 1 layout (pass 2)")

- **A creek** across the zone that drains into the graveyard (one flooded corner, why it's "sunken"). The **old bridge** on the main road is the doorway to Wolfwood, where the safe part ends; stepping stones carry the shortcut over it.
- **Rest spots** with no mobs: the crossroads campfire and a **hunter's camp** in Wolfwood, a base for levels 5–7.
- **Three loops:** (1) crossroads → graveyard → a forest path north into Wolfwood → main road home; (2) down the sinkhole → Old Gnawbone → climb out a **back hole** → shortcut south; (3) chapel → shortcut down the east side.

## Wolfwood brief (draft 1, Nov 19; owner answers below)
Step 1 of `area-rules.md`. Nothing drawn or built yet.
- **Levels:** 8–11 (draft 2 ladder). 8 at the doors, 10–11 deepest (north, toward the chapel). Greyfang is the fight for 11–12; Old Scar (rare) 11. The cap is still 10, so the top end can't be fully beaten until the skill tree raises it.
- **Theme:** old dark forest going sick. Leafy dusk trees at the crossroads side turn into tall dark firs; grey blight creeps out from the dead giant tree.
- **Mood words:** hunted, hushed, old.
- **Landmark:** the dead giant tree, grey and bare above the canopy, seen from the crossroads bridge and the graveyard's north gate.
- **Ways in (three, all already pointing here):** crossroads north road (main, blended border: the forest darkens over a stretch of road), graveyard north gate (hard cut: the fence), crypt back tunnel (comes up in a hillside, deep in). North exit to the chapel stays a stub until the chapel is built.
- **Named:** Greyfang wanders a loop path round the middle of the wood with 2 pack wolves. Howls to call the pack back, never flees. No summon key (wanders on a timer). First wandering named in the game.
- **Rare:** Old Scar, one-eyed grey wolf, torn ear. Alone, hits hard.
- **Things to find:** the hunter's abandoned camp (rest spot, no mobs: cold firepit, lean-to, drying rack, sprung traps); the giant tree's hollow (hidden chest); Greyfang's den (bones, a gnawed hunter's bow); the crypt tunnel's mouth (loop back to the graveyard); a deer trail side path.
- **Mobs:** blight wolves in packs of 2–3 that flee at low health (and can drag in more if you let them run); proposed second mob for variety: one solo puller between packs (owner to pick: forest spider that webs/roots you, or a thornback boar that charges).
- **Look:** dim green-grey light, shafts of light in clearings, low fog between trunks, needles and moss on the ground, blight patches near the giant tree. Critters: crows, moths, a deer that bolts.
- **New:** wolf body (blight wolf, Greyfang, Old Scar), fir trees and the dead giant tree, needle/moss/blight ground, hunter's camp and den props, a 'wood' tint, wandering named with a pack, wolves fleeing at low health.

## Ruined Chapel brief (draft 1, Nov 20; owner answers below)
Step 1 of `area-rules.md`. Nothing drawn or built yet. "Ruined Chapel" means the **chapel grounds** (outdoor, shared). The chapel building itself is the door to **Dungeon 1** (Malrec's instance, designed in `design.md`; its first room is the nave).
- **Levels:** 10–14 (draft 2 ladder). 10 where Wolfwood's north road comes in, 12–13 round the cult camps, 14 on the chapel steps. The Ashen Acolyte (rare) is 14. The cap is still 10, so like Wolfwood most of it waits for the cap to rise; build to the ladder anyway.
- **Theme:** a forest monastery gone wrong. The firs open into a walled clearing: a ruined chapel with a tall steeple, a roofless cloister, an overgrown herb garden, a small monks' plot by the wall. The fen cultists have moved in: candles everywhere, ash circles, grey smoke from censers, cult banners nailed over the old ones.
- **Mood words:** smoky, devout, wrong.
- **Landmark:** the steeple above the trees with **one lit window**, seen from Wolfwood's north road (the window is the breadcrumb). Its bell has fallen and lies cracked in the cloister (unlike the graveyard's broken bell tower, this one is whole; only the bell is down).
- **Ways in:** Wolfwood's north road (main, soft border: the firs thin, the needles turn to trampled ash and gravel, the light warms with candle glow). **New: the east shortcut** (already in `design.md`): a steep path down the east side back to the crossroads, so you can walk home without retracing Wolfwood. Opens from the chapel side only (a gate you unbar), so it's a reward, not a way to skip Wolfwood.
- **Named:** none outside. **Brother Malrec** is inside the dungeon (levels 14–15).
- **Rare:** the **Ashen Acolyte** (14): grey robes trailing smoke, casts a shadow bolt (telegraphed, like the King's), and at low health **flees to the chapel door**; if he reaches it he heals up and comes back. Snare, root or stun him.
- **Mobs:** **fen cultists** in **camps of three** that **call for help** (one shout pulls the next camp if it's close): a **zealot** (melee), an **ashcaller** (caster) and a **chanter** that buffs the others. That teaches "kill the support first" before Malrec's healers. Second mob for variety (owner to pick, see questions).
- **Things to find:** the **fallen bell** in the cloister (hidden chest under its lip); the **wild herb garden** (side path, a quiet corner); the **ash circle**, the cult's ritual spot (a cultist camp in it at the top of the band); a **pilgrims' wayside shrine** on the road in, with candles still burning (rest spot like the hunter's camp); the **east gate** (unbar it for the shortcut home).
- **Look (for the style takes):** warm candle and ember light against cold dusk, grey smoke drifting low instead of fog, ash and gravel ground inside the walls, broken flagstones, ivy on stone. Critters: crows on the wall, moths at the candles.
- **New:** chapel and steeple (outside only), cloister walls and arches, fallen bell, herb garden, ash circle, candles and censers, cult banners, wayside shrine, east gate, flagstone/ash/gravel ground, a 'chapel' tint, cultist bodies (3 kinds), the Acolyte, "call for help" between camps, a buffing mob, a flee-to-heal rare, the dungeon door (stub until Dungeon 1 is built).
- **Reused:** fir trees at the south edge (where it meets Wolfwood), the road, chest, rares, map and minimap, telegraph bars (the King's bolt), campfire rest bonus.
- **Size:** about 48 × 40 tiles, north of Wolfwood; the east path runs down to the crossroads' east side.
- **Owner answers (Nov 20):** second mob is the **ash wisp** (floating candle flame, drains mana, blinks around). The **east shortcut opening from the chapel side only** is OK. **No flee-to-heal on the Ashen Acolyte**: some classes can stop a runner and some can't, so it's unfair; he keeps the telegraphed shadow bolt, and his own move gets picked at the style takes. **On hold** until the skill tree raises the cap past 10 (owner expects that by the next map chat); then style takes.
- **Style takes drawn (Nov 20):** canvas "Mobile MMO — Ruined Chapel style takes" (A Vesper embers: cold dusk with warm candle pools, the brief as written; B Ash fall: grey-brown, ash in the air, red embers; C Night vigil: deep blue night, only candles warm; D Fen rot: murky green-gold, sickly candles). Drawn by `tools/chapel_takes.py`. **Owner picked A · Vesper embers (Nov 20);** look sheet in `area-rules.md`.
- **Ashen Acolyte's move (owner to pick, replaces flee-to-heal):** (1) **Ember Brand:** a telegraphed ring of embers under you (1.5 s); step out or take a big burn. Every class can walk, so it's fair. (2) **Ash Veil:** at half health he bursts into smoke and reappears a few tiles away, leaving a smoke cloud where he stood that makes you miss more while you stand in it; walk out and chase. (3) **Censer Smoke:** every so often he swings his censer and grey smoke spreads round him; while he stands in it he takes less damage, so you pull him out of it or wait it out. Leaning (1): simple, readable on a phone, and a new skill (moving) the King's bolt doesn't teach.
