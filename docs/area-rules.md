# Area rules: how we build a new area

The baseline for every new area (owner, Nov 18, 2026). Add to it as we build. Map research and the "why" behind most of these: `docs/research/map-design.md`.

## How a new area gets made (one area per pass)
1. **Brief** (in chat, short): levels, theme, three mood words, landmark, named, rare, 3–5 things to find, which mobs live there, and a list of what's new vs reused.
2. **Style takes** (only when it's a new setting): 3–4 takes of the same scene in different looks. The owner picks one before anything else gets drawn.
3. **Full area mock-up:** one big top-down map of the whole area (the owner zooms in instead of walking it), plus one in-game phone view. Big chunks are fine; one area at a time. Since Nov 18 this is a text map in `js/data/maps/` that must pass `tools/mapcheck.py` before the owner sees it (`docs/map-format.md`); the checker draws both pictures.
4. **Owner notes**, then fixes. New rules that come out of the notes go into this file.
5. **Build it in the game**, then play-test on the phone.

## Every area feels like its own place
- **Own look:** its own light tint, fog, ground, plants, props, critters and sounds. You should know where you are from a single screenshot.
- **Dead areas are dead all the way, inside their border.** A graveyard gets dead trees, dead grass and bare branches behind its gate; the forest outside can stay green right up to it. Plants, trees and ground always match the area you're standing in.
- **New setting, new assets.** Draw new props, trees and tiles that fit the area instead of recolouring the last area's. Reuse is fine only when it truly belongs (the same road, the same fence where two areas meet, rats in more than one cave). It should be hard to spot repeats.
- **Areas in a zone are related, zones are different (owner, Nov 19).** Areas inside one zone feel similar but not the same: they share the zone's family of light, palette and mood, and each still has its own setting (Zone 1: the graveyard is cold and dead, the rat tunnel damp earth, Wolfwood foresty with wolves, all dusky and moody). A **new zone** is where the look changes up for real: new light, palette and mood.
- **One landmark you can see (or hear, or see lit up) from the area's entrance.**
- **Borders fit the place (owner, Nov 18).** Pick the transition that makes sense. **Hard cut** where there's a real boundary: a gate, a wall, a fence, a cave mouth (walk through the graveyard gate and now you're in a graveyard: dead trees, dead grass, new light, all at once). **Gradual blend** where open land meets open land, and in bigger areas: the forest thins out, grass dries, the light shifts over a stretch of road so it flows naturally. Default to the blend outdoors; use the hard cut when something physical marks the line.

## Look at references first; every object must read (owner, Nov 19)
Some art hasn't read as what it was meant to be (the crypt bones looked like little white blobs; the first Wolfwood lean-to looked like an upside-down pyramid). So for every new prop, monster or tile:
- **Look at references before drawing.** Pull a few reference images with image search, and always run these searches (owner, Nov 25): **"pixel <thing>"** (pixel crypt, pixel coffin, pixel cobweb), **"video game <thing>"** (video game crypt), and real photos of it. The same goes for a whole area before its layout or look ("pixel crypt", "video game crypt" before the crypt rework). Then, and note the 2–3 shapes that make it recognisable (a lean-to = sloped roof + ridge pole + posts + dark opening; a bone = long shaft + knobbed ends, a skull = round dome + dark eye holes).
- **Draw those shapes big and simple.** At game size a thing reads by its outline, so lead with silhouette and contrast, not small detail.
- **Squint test at real game zoom** before showing or shipping: crop it from an in-game screenshot at the phone's zoom and ask "would someone name this right without being told?" If not, redraw.
- **When it's new or unsure, show it first:** a crop of the object in game next to one reference image, so the owner can say yes before it ships.

## Heart: the small details (owner, Nov 20)
The goal is a game that feels thought about, not generated. Small details set games apart, so every area gets them on purpose:
- **Something alive in every screen:** a critter, a flicker, a ripple, smoke, a moving plant. Frogs by water, crows on walls, moths at lights, owls in trees.
- **Wear and age:** moss and ivy on old stone (the bell tower's moss is the bar), cracks, stains, worn paths, chipped edges.
- **Right sizes:** doors, windows and props sized for the 20 × 24 characters, not left over from older art (the graveyard shack's door is too small).
- **Small stories:** a prop that hints at what happened (the dead adventurer in the rat tunnel, the hunter's camp, the fallen bell).
- **Ways between areas look like something:** an entrance where something marks it (gate, cave mouth, stairs), or a path that just carries on into the next place; never a black box at the edge.
- **Take it slow:** art passes go in chunks, one area at a time, and each chunk gets looked at on the phone before the next.
- **Learned on the graveyard pass (v0.49):** every stone weathers on its own (lean, chip, crack, moss on the top and foot, lichen), never all the same; critters get a lighter back or pale eyes so they show against dark ground on a phone; a critter that hides when you come close (crows, frogs) makes the place feel aware of you; dead areas get dead reeds and leaves too, not just dead grass; the area's name should show up in the art (sunken headstones in the Sunken Graveyard's water). Critters go in the map file as `critter` lines (`map-format.md`).

- **Learned on the crossroads pass (v0.59):** **roads line up** (owner: the paths after the bridge didn't meet): a road keeps one line through a bridge, a gate or a crossing; a one-tile jog right at a bridge or a junction looks like a mistake, so shift the whole run instead. A grand road is cobbled (`=`), a branch to somewhere rough (a hole, a camp) is a worn dirt trail (`:`). **Enough mobs** (owner, Nov 20: it's an MMO, players need things to kill): fill the little clearings in the trees with spawns, low levels near the way in, a step up farther out, never so many that a new player gets overrun. **A way to a place that isn't built yet still looks like an entrance** (Town 1's gate, doors open; the chat says it isn't built). Put an edge entrance a couple of tiles in from the map edge so the camera shows it.
- **How the new pieces are drawn (v0.59):** *wagon*: a planked box tipped toward its missing front wheel, pale canvas on dark hoops sagging between them with a tear, the back wheel as a rim + six spokes + hub, the lost wheel as a squashed ring in the grass, a split crate and a sack. *Town gate*: two 7 px timber posts 40 px tall, a roofed beam, doors drawn edge-on swinging inward, stakes of slightly uneven height, a lantern per post, a shield sign. *Fishing rod*: a thin rod from the rail out over the water, a faint line and a red float, a bucket. *Signpost*: capped post, one arm per road, one hanging crooked by a nail. *Bridge*: about one plank in eight gone (dark water and a glint show through). *Firefly*: a 1 px yellow-green dot with a soft plus-shaped glow, drifting slowly and fading in and out, drawn after the lighting so it glows. *Rabbit*: 8 × 6, brown with a lighter back and a white tail, nibbles and hops, bolts away from you and vanishes into the trees.

- **Learned designing the chapel grounds and Dungeon 1 (Nov 20, the first areas built to the checklist from day one):** **check the landmark on the phone view from the way in**, not just the overview: draft 1 of the chapel had the steeple out of the first screen, so the road was moved to come up under it. **Plan the 1–3 small stories in the brief** and give each a prop that tells it without words (graves dug up + the spade left in them; books burned in a heap before the door; the old banner torn down under the new one; a pilgrim's pack dropped and left). **Water gets life** even in a dark place (frogs at the monks' pond). **A path ends somewhere:** no worn path or flagstones running into a wall. **Dungeons follow the same checklist:** one map walked straight through (no loading between rooms), easy at the door and deeper is harder, a secret off the main way that loops back, a small story per room, light that changes room to room (stained glass in the nave, braziers below), something alive (bats, moths) in every room.

## Living detail: small stories and life (owner, Nov 25; research in `docs/research/map-design.md`, "Living detail")
Owner: make the world feel alive and dynamic, down to the smallest details. Every area (and every area pass) gets:
- **2–3 small stories told by 2–4 props that only make sense together.** No sign explains it; the player joins the dots (a torn cloak + a snapped bow + prints walking away = the hunter didn't make it). Put them on paths players already walk (a named's route, the road, the way to a chest).
- **A story can be alive:** something still going on (a fire still kept, a full cup nobody drank, a bell still swinging), not only things left behind.
- **Warn before you show:** details thicken toward danger (bones and toadstools toward the blight, prints and torn cloth before a den, scorch marks before a fire-caster).
- **Everyday props say who uses the place:** tools left where work stopped (a saw in the cut, a spade in the grave), stacks, carts, cups. Abandoned places have no light; used places do.
- **Something alive on every phone screen**, and at least one thing that **reacts to you** (crows fly, frogs plop, rabbits and deer bolt, eyes blink out, the owl turns). New critters: **deer** (v0.81: grazes, looks up and watches you inside about 6 tiles, bounds away inside about 3.5, comes back once you've gone) and **eyes** (v0.82, owner: two floating dots didn't read): **a watcher, not dots**. A low dark head-and-shoulders shape with two ears, just darker than the trunks behind it, and two almond eyes of pale gold with a bright core toward the nose and a soft halo (drawn with `screen` after the lighting); it blinks and tilts its head; come within about 5 tiles and the eyes flick sideways and go out while the shape slinks back into the trees. Place it **on open ground right at a treeline, firs behind it (north) and open ground in front**, never on top of a crown. Eyeshine is **never red**: red stays for real monsters.
- **Critters go where they show:** not under a crown or behind a trunk (firs hide whatever stands just north of them). Deer and rabbits in clearings and glades, crows on branch tips or bones, eyes under the darkest firs.
- **Check every new prop on the in-game phone view, zoomed crop next to the game** (the saw first floated above its log, the log ends read as boxes, the deer bones hid behind firs: all caught that way).

## Light with a reason (owner, Nov 25: "make sure the lights make sense, tell a story, give flavor")
- **Every light has a source you can see** (fire, lantern, candle, window, crack of sky, glowing fungus), sized to it: a candle lights a step, a brazier a room, a campfire a camp.
- **Two colours and an accent per area:** warm (fire, lamps, dusk sun) against cold (crypt light, moon, foxfire), plus one accent at most (the stained glass's colours, the cult's red). Never plain white.
- **Light tells who's here:** a place still in use is lit (the cult keeps its braziers fed, the hunter keeps his fire); a place that was given up is dark (the woodcutters' stack). A light that shouldn't be there is a story too.
- **Rhythm, not even brightness:** dark between pools, but **always one pool in view ahead** along the main way, a little warmer toward the way on. Dark side passages are a choice, never the only way.
- **Light obeys walls (v0.95, owner Nov 27: "make sure the physics make sense").** Light spreads across open floor and never shines through rock: a room is lit by its own lights, not by the room next door. A lamp hung on a wall lights the floor in front of it, not the other side. Wall faces catch light only on the side facing the light.
- **Indoors, light fills the room (v0.95).** Each light has its bright core plus a long faint tail (about 2.6× its radius) that fills its room and creeps a little down halls. So: one brazier lights a mid-size room; don't add a second just to beat the dark. The dark gaps between rooms stay. Outdoors has no tail; the open-air look stays as it is (owner likes it).
- *How it's drawn (v0.95, `bakeLight` in js/area.js):* when an area is built, each fixed light walks outward over open tiles (walls = tiles tagged `wall`), and the walked distance sets how bright each spot is (straight-line distance where the floor is in plain sight, so pools stay round). Brightness = core (as before: (1 − d/r)², squashed 1.15× up and down for the 3/4 view) + tail (`spill` × (1 − d/(r × `reach`))^1.6). Indoor tints (crypt, earth, vault): spill 0.35, reach 2.6; outdoor tints: spill 0. Baked into two pictures at half resolution (darkness lifted, firelight colour), softened 6 px, drawn each frame. Fire flicker = a small warm glow (0.45 × radius) near each flame. Lights that move (floating mobs, hazards, glowing named) stay live stamps.
- **Rest spots are the warmest light in an area** (campfire, shrine): the refuge.
- **Dungeons:** dark is pools, not black. Every room gets at least one light that says something about it (who lit it, why), and the way to the next room is lit.
- **Indoor lights are placed like a builder placed them (owner, Nov 27, lighting pass v0.96):** wall torches, braziers and fire pillars come in **mirrored pairs**, mirrored on the room's door or its middle line (pillars, thrones and doors are the usual axis), at the same row. A long hall gets evenly spaced pairs. **Exceptions that tell a story are fine:** a single candle someone left (the robbers' candle, the chest's candle), a mourner's shrine, a ruin with only one brazier still going. Outdoors is looser: a lantern where someone would hang one (gate, camp, shack). **One light per spot:** no spare candle beside a shrine that already has its own. *How to check:* `python3 tools/light_overview.py out/lights <area>` draws the whole map with its baked light and numbers every light; `tools/mapcheck.py` warns on two lights in one spot (same-row pairs and pairs flanking a light pass).
- *Wall torch (`sconce`, v0.96; refs: "pixel wall torch", "video game wall sconce")*: goes **on a north wall tile** (the face you see in the 3/4 view; side walls get standing braziers or candles instead, since we never see their face). An iron plate (6 × 6 with a lit top edge and a rivet) pinned low on the face, a short wooden torch (2 px, lit left edge) leaning out from it with a black-edged iron cup on top; the flame is drawn live. Light: radius 50, warm 32, starting on the floor just in front of the wall so it never starts inside rock. In the crypt (thin wall face) the torch head stands above the wall line; in Dungeon 1's taller dressed-stone walls it sits on the face.
- **Canopy sunbeams: cut (v0.82.1, owner Nov 25: "light still doesn't read, cut it").** Two tries (v0.81 band, v0.82 flecks and shafts) didn't read on the phone, so Wolfwood has none. Don't add sunbeams to an area again without showing the owner a crop first. Old recipe kept for reference:
  - **How canopy sunbeams were drawn (v0.82; owner: v0.81's single faint band didn't read):** what makes sun through trees read is the **sun flecks**: a dapple of 14 small bright gold spots (1–3 px) scattered in an ellipse about 34 × 16 px where the light lands, each fading in and out on its own like leaves moving, over a faint warm pool. Above it **three crisp parallel shafts** (3, 5 and 2 px wide, gaps between) slanting down from the upper left over 88 px, brightest at the ground and fading to nothing toward the canopy, each breathing on its own; six dust motes twinkle down inside them. Drawn after the lighting with `screen` so they brighten what's under them. Warm light 46 px. Where: canopy gaps, more at a wood's edge and in glades, none in its darkest part.
- *Foxfire* (v0.81, object; **cut from Wolfwood in v0.88**, owner: it didn't read, the green dots on a stub looked like bugs): a rotting stub of dark wood furred with pale green dots that pulse slowly after the lighting, a mote drifting up now and then; a small cold light (22 px). It belongs on rot: fallen logs, stumps, the edge of blight.

## Towns are hubs (owner, Nov 27)
- A town is a small Diablo-style hub, not a city to explore: everyone the player needs stands round **one square** within a few seconds' walk of the start (hearth, smith, gem cutter, bank, peddler, quest giver). No hunting through streets on a phone. Houses behind the square only give the sense of a town. Quest steps are turned in through the quest giver's orb from anywhere, so nobody walks back just to turn in.
- **Town 1 look (v0.143):** same dusk as the crossroads. Buildings face south into the square; each reads by 2–3 shapes: forge = open shed on posts + glowing stone hearth + anvil; bank = dressed stone + two pillars + iron-bound door + gold coin sign; townhouse = white plaster between dark timbers + shingle roof + chimney; wizard tower = round stone tower + tall pointed blue roof with gold stars + glowing windows; gem table = striped awning + sparkling gems + lamp; scrying orb = stone pedestal + cold blue orb (kept clear of the tower's roof tip). Roofs use one shingle helper (`townRoof`). **A walled town needs about 8 tiles of forest outside its wall**, because the ground past a map edge is a mirror of the 16 tiles inside it and would copy the square.
- **Town 1 draft 2 (v0.148, owner Nov 27: round, cozier, more flavour):** a town is **round**: a circle of palisade (wall tiles = inside tiles with any of their 8 neighbours outside, so the ring has no diagonal gaps), a round flagstone square in the middle with the hearth at its heart, every service on a ring a few steps out, worn dirt paths (`:`) from the square to each door. Keep the farthest service about 5 s from the hearth. Flavour is small working things where they'd be: benches by the fire, barrels by the smith, hay and a trough with the horses, smoke from every chimney. **Stable recipe:** long low plank barn (vertical planks, dark frame posts, stone footing) under golden straw thatch with a ragged eave; a big X-braced double door in the middle; a stall either side with a half door and a horse's long face (ears, pale blaze, eyes, nostrils) over it; a hay-loft hatch in the thatch with hay piled in it and stalks hanging over the sill (hay sticking up reads as a sign, not hay); a hanging sign on an iron bracket. **Paddock:** split-rail fence (tile `j`): two rails on one post per tile across the screen, a narrow rail strip with posts up and down it, rails butt into the palisade. **Horses** (critter `horse`, 24 × 18, drawn at size): long face, mane, slim legs, tail; they stay tame (look at you, never flee) and keep off the fence and trough. Keep critters' feet off objects: a horse standing on a trough's tile reads wrong.

## Natural placement, no mistakes
- **Every repeated prop comes in versions (owner, Nov 25: "different versions of each of these things, kind of like broken pillars are different"):** when an area has two or more of the same kind of prop (open coffins, broken pillars, urns, bone heaps, skull piles), each one is drawn differently: a different break, lean, damage, what's spilled or left on it, so no two read as copies. Pick the version from the prop's position (`ah(o.x,o.y,..)`) or an option word on its map line (`obj open_coffin 21 24 cracked`), and give each kind at least 3 versions. Check the area on the phone view for any two that look the same.
- **Trees in front of a wall are fine; trees on a wall are not (owner, Nov 27, love pass review).** In the 3/4 view a tree standing a tile south of a wall or a path draws its crown over it, and that reads right. What reads wrong is a **trunk** standing on the wall line, the gatehouse or the path itself. Check where the trunk foot is, not where the crown reaches.
- Everything looks like it would really be there. Nothing overlaps what it shouldn't: no gravestones on a tower, no props in walls, holes or each other, no mobs standing in scenery.
- Every object has a footprint (the space it takes up); buildings and paths keep about a tile of space around them.
- Things sit where they'd really be: graves in rows on open ground, rubble at the foot of what it fell from, paths worn where people walk, puddles in low spots.
- Buildings have believable openings: doors at ground level, windows only where a building would have them.
- Every mock-up gets checked for overlaps before the owner sees it (`tools/mapcheck.py` does this for map files). When a new rule can be checked by a script, add it to the checker too.

## Natural shapes: nothing looks like the tile grid (owner, Nov 20)
The map is made of square tiles, but the world shouldn't look like it. Square water in the crossroads and the graveyard read almost like a path. These rules apply to every area, old and new:
- **Water has natural edges:** banks curve and bite across tile lines, corners are rounded, never a straight run longer than a couple of tiles. A darker wet rim at the edge, then mud, stones and reeds; shallows fade in before the deep water. Creeks wander and change width; ponds are blobs, not boxes.
- **How water is drawn (v0.52, the recipe for every area):** water is one smooth shape, not tiles: a little blur turns stair steps into slopes, two layers of slow wobble bend the banks (a few pixels either way), so a 1-tile creek varies in width and a pond is a blob. From land to deep: wet mud strip (changing width) → 1 px dark wet rim → lighter, greener shallows → mid → deep in soft patches, with a shadow under the north bank (3/4 view). Then the bank details: pebbles at the waterline, reeds in clumps (dead and tan in dead places), lily pads only where the water is alive, light catching the deep. Water never creeps onto a road or a built floor. Anything placed in water (headstones, critters) checks the drawn shape, not the tile.
- **Every edge between two grounds is soft:** grass into dirt, path into grass, moss into blight: ragged, uneven lines, never a ruler edge along the tiles.
- **Woods are mixed:** never one tree repeated. 2–3 kinds per area growing in stands (neighbours mostly match, a few mixed in), young, mid and old sizes, small shade shifts from crown to crown, and something growing between the trunks (seedlings, ferns, fallen branches). Each area sets its mix in `TREE_MIX` (`js/area.js`), or gets new kinds when the setting calls for it (v0.51).
- **How ground edges are drawn (v0.54, the recipe for every area):** each pixel picks its ground from a smooth blend of the tiles around it, plus a little wobble, so two grounds meet in loose curves. **Natural ground** (grass, dead grass, mud, marsh, needles, moss, blight) blends loosely; **worn paths** get a small head start so a thin path stays joined at diagonal steps; **built ground** (roads, floors, stairs, flagstones) keeps a near-straight edge, because people made it (owner, Nov 20: a built path can be straight). Colour patches inside a ground are wobbled so they never form a checker grid. Small details (tufts, pebbles) only sit on the ground that actually shows there.
- **Past the map's edge (owner, Nov 20):** no big black boxes. Fill in with what's around: earth and dirt in a cave (the rat tunnel's walls are the model), trees where a forest runs off the map, fields past open land. Card: "Zone edges and ways between areas".
- **The woods carry on past the edge (v0.64, owner: "the trees show a definitive line of where the map actually stops"):** the strip past a map's top and bottom edge (v0.55) gets trees too, up to 8 rows deep (`edgeWoods` in `js/area.js`, outdoor areas only). **Recipe:** the edge's own trees carry on outward (one in ten left out, so it never reads as a mirror), so roads and clearings that run off the map stay open; further out the gaps close up into deeper forest (more trees each row), in the kind of tree most common along that edge; now and then a dead snag. Their shadows go into the mirrored ground strip. Nothing out there can be walked on.
- **Walls round a town are palisades (v0.64, Town 1 at the crossroads):** tile `L`. **Recipe:** sharpened logs four to a tile, each a little different in height with a point and the odd knot, a lashing rail across, earth banked at the foot with a bit of moss; it runs off into the trees either side so the wall feels like it goes on.
- **Fences and walls join up (owner, Nov 20: fences that don't connect on the turns feel weird):** a fence line turns with a square corner, never a diagonal staircase of tiles (posts that only touch at their corners float). In the map file every fence tile in a run touches the next one side to side; the checker's fence list (or a quick script) shows any tile with fewer than two side neighbours. **How fences are drawn (v0.56, the recipe):** bars stand only where the rails run, so nothing sticks out past a turn or an end; every corner, end or T gets **one thick post** (stone foot, capped top) that both runs meet; straight runs show bars and two rails across the screen, a post column up and down it. Same idea for any later wall, hedge or rope line.
- **Old fences are worn, not perfect (owner, Nov 20: "a fallen fence, fence links bent"):** on about one panel in four something is off: a **bent** bar (kinked over above the top rail), a bar **snapped** short, or one **gone**; now and then a spear tip knocked off true, a top rail that **sags**, rust running down. **Gaps** (`/`) are where a panel came down: what stood next to it ends in a **snapped stub**, and the panel **lies flat outside the line** where it fell (a short ladder on the ground, one bar bent up off it); in a gap two tiles long only one tile holds the panel, the other a loose bar. Where a creek runs out under the fence, the fence keeps standing in the water and a **fallen panel lies half-sunk** in the creek just outside, the current lapping over it. Keep it to a few per area so it reads as age, not ruin.
- **Water you can wade (v0.64, owner: the crossroads creek, a teaser for Dungeon 1's flood):** `wade: 1` on a map makes its `~` water walkable at 60% speed for you and mobs. Drawn the same; anyone wading sinks to the shins: a 5 px band of the water's colour over the feet with a moving light ripple and two glints either side (`render.js`). Floating things skim over. Water you can't wade stops your **feet**, not a point: a few pixels either side and in front, so half your body never hangs out over a pond.
- **Lights never shine through a tree (v0.64):** a torch or lantern flame under a tree crown that stands in front of it isn't drawn (it flickered through the leaves). Better still, keep lights 2+ tiles clear of trees on the side the crown hangs over (trees grow up and out from the trunk).
- **Arriving through a way in (v0.64):** you come out on open ground, never in a pocket: a spot needs most of the 5 × 5 tiles round it open (`areaSpot`, `js/world.js`). Don't put posts, lanterns or bushes right next to an exit.
- **How cave walls are drawn (v0.74, the rat tunnel; the recipe for any dug or natural cave):** like water, the walls are one shape, not tiles (`paintEarthWalls` in `js/area.js`): a smoothed wall field (tile centres blended, sampled in a small square) plus two layers of wobble decides wall or floor pixel by pixel, so edges curve and corners round off. The middle of every tile keeps its own kind, so the drawn edge never strays more than a few pixels from where you can walk. Then the 3/4 view: a **soil face 10 px tall** wherever floor lies just below (dark line at the foot, a lighter lip on top, one wavy darker layer across it, specks), with stones, hanging roots and rat holes placed on the drawn foot, not the tile line; a **lit lip** on far (north) rims, a thin rim on the sides, a **damp shadow** on the floor at the foot, pale **mushrooms** there. Drawn before the edge copies, so past the map's edge it's solid earth. Built walls (crypt masonry, chapel stone) stay straight; only natural rock and earth get this.
- **Drawing, not map changes:** these are fixed in how tiles are drawn, so map files, walking and saves stay the same.
- **Check it on the in-game view:** if a shape looks like it follows the grid, redraw it.

## Area pass checklist (owner, Nov 20)
Every built area gets a pass with this list, and every new area (the Ruined Chapel grounds, Dungeon 1, Zone 2) is built with it from day one. Tick each line on the in-game view on a phone-sized screen:
1. **Natural shapes:** water and ground edges use the recipes above; walls, cliffs and cave edges don't follow tile steps; woods are mixed.
2. **Edges and ways out:** no black past the map edge; every way out looks like what it is (gate, cave mouth, stairs, or a path that carries on).
3. **Heart:** something alive in every screen; wear and age on anything old; doors and props sized for people; 1–3 small stories.
4. **Reads at a glance:** every prop and monster passes the squint test (references first).
5. **Natural placement:** nothing overlaps, things sit where they'd really be (`tools/mapcheck.py`); fences and walls join at square corners with a post.
6. **Its own place:** the area matches its look sheet below, and its light and fog change where the place changes.
7. **Saved:** every decision and recipe from the pass goes into this file in the same push, and the reply says what was saved.

## Every look decision gets saved here (owner, Nov 20)
Whenever the owner makes a call on how things should look or feel (in any chat), write it into this file in the section it fits, with the date, in the same push. This file is how every chat keeps the world consistent; the board only tracks the work. **Also save the recipe (owner, Nov 20):** when a visual change ships, add a short "how it's drawn" line here (like the water and ground recipes above), so every new location is built the same way from day one. Each reply that changes a visual says which rule it saved. This is the best-practice file for every new location.

## Boss moments: how they're drawn (v0.79, Dungeon 1 boss pass)
- **A named that holds the way on** shows it: the Prior's stairs sit under dark flood water; the Bellringer's passage has an **iron gate** (rusty bars 4 px apart, two riveted crossbars, spiked feet in the floor, a dark slot in the arch above). It rises into the arch over about 1.4 s when he's driven off, the same way the Prior's portcullis lifts.

## Loot moments: how they're drawn (v0.97, Items lane)

## Characters and monsters: size (owner, Nov 21)
- **Player characters are 15 × 18 on screen (final).** How it's drawn: each class is authored at 20 × 24 in `RAGS` (`js/data/sprites.js`) and the renderer drops every 4th row and column (`js/render.js`) to show it at 15 × 18. New class looks are drawn the same way.

## Monster bodies: recipe from the undead pass (owner OK, Nov 21)
- **Each family gets its own body**, never a recolour of another family. Variants of one family (crypt skeleton, Necro pet, Shadow Knight minion) share the body and change bone colour, eye colour and what they hold.
- **Size against the 15 × 18 player:** a regular humanoid monster is a touch taller (16 × 20); a boss or dungeon boss is about 1.6× the player (24 × 28–30), drawn at full detail, never a small sprite scaled up.
- **How it's drawn:** fill-only text grids with the black outline added by a script (`tools/undead_mobs.py`, `outline()`), light from the top left, faces right, one step frame where the legs (or the hem, for robes) move.
- **The shapes that make each read:** skeleton = skull with dark sockets and a glint, ribs as white bars split by black, a spine, a pelvis, thin limbs with gaps; zombie = hunched forward, both arms reaching, jaw hanging, skin showing through torn clothes, one bare foot; Skeleton King = crown, glowing sockets, fur collar, torn royal cloak, a big sword he leans on.
- **Named look bigger and shaggier than their pack (owner, v0.67):** a named is about 1.5–2× its family's size with fur tufts on the back, ragged edges and one story prop on the body (Gnawbone's bone and trap jaw, Greyfang's arrows and scars), so it reads as old and dangerous, not a recolour.
- **A boss stands apart from the mobs around it:** Brother Malrec keeps his cult's hood but in grand vestments (tall gold-tipped cowl, gold collar, gold-trimmed red robe and stole, gold hem, a crook-staff with a lit censer), so he reads as the one in charge at a glance.
- **Regular mobs: recipe from the redesign pass (owner OK, v0.70):** a family shares one hand-drawn body and each role tells its job with one thing in hand or on the robe. *Cult body* (15 × 19 fill + outline): a rounded cowl lit on its left rim, the face in shadow with two glowing eyes, a short mantle over the shoulders, a sleeve with a hand showing, a rope belt with a knotted tail, two or three fold lines, a darker hem band, boots peeping out that swap on the step; keep the flare (narrow hood, wide hem), a boxy body loses the family look. Zealot sickle, ashcaller an ember cupped up, chanter a swinging censer, mender an open book and a bandage roll, summoner a coal held high with an ember rune on the hem, deacon a size up with a gold stole and a mace, the Ashen Acolyte taller with pale robes going sooty at the hem and smoke rising. *Beasts:* a size under their named (the blight wolf 37 × 18 next to Greyfang's 38 × 21, the big tunnel rat a small Gnawbone), fur as a lighter back over a darker belly, one story mark (the blight patch, Old Scar's long scar and milky eye, the boar's thorn ridge). A rare that shares a body gets its own colourway (the Pale Rat).
- **Legs and feet (owner, Nov 21, from the redesign pass):** feet point the way the creature faces (facing right, toes and paws stick out to the right, never back toward the tail). **Rats:** short legs straight down with a small pink foot tucked under the body, like Gnawbone and the small rats; no splayed toes. Legs come in pairs set back from the head (the front pair under the shoulders, never under the head), and a big rat has a full belly so the legs sit naturally under it (v0.70.1). **Wolf heads (owner, v0.70.1: the first redo read as a weird dog):** a flatter skull with a sloping brow, tall pointed ears set back, the eye high under the brow, and a long tapered muzzle with a dark nose tip; a round head and short snout read as a dog. **Wolves:** front legs straight, hind legs bend at the hock (the joint points back, the leg below it comes forward), paws two pixels long reaching forward. **Boar:** sturdy legs tapering to small dark hooves straight under them. **Walk frames:** move each whole leg a pixel, every other leg forward and back, so the pairs splay; never shift single pixels (it splits two-pixel legs in half). **Bugs have feelers:** a beetle gets two thin feelers off the head, curving up and forward, a pixel apart so they read as two lines (a dotted pair reads as a block). **Golems and elementals are symmetric** left to right; only the light (from the left) differs.
- **Named in a family get their own model too (owner OK, v0.69):** the Drowned Prior and the Bellringer keep the cult's hood (so they read as monastery) but each gets one story told in its shape: the Prior's soaked hood slumps and shows his drowned face (bloated grey-green, sunken sockets, pale glowing eyes), weed hangs off a habit that's darker and wetter below the waist, the hem drips, and his dead lantern glows faintly; the Bellringer is a hunched hulk (hood set low and forward between big shoulders, a dark line round the hood so the head doesn't melt into the back), linen ear rags, a foundry apron with a burn hole, a handbell on the rope belt, bare fists, the bell's iron clapper as a maul. Sizes: just under the dungeon boss (Prior 25 × 29, Bellringer 28 × 29, the widest), so the boss stays the biggest thing in the dungeon.

## Player bodies: recipe from the player pass (owner OK as placeholders, v0.68, Nov 21)
- **Draw at the real size, 15 × 18,** pixel by pixel: a 13 × 16 fill plus the black outline from `outline()` (`tools/player_rags.py`). Never draw bigger and shrink; dropped rows and columns cut eyes and break outlines.
- **Layout:** head 6–7 rows (about 40% of the height) centred on columns 3–8, body columns 2–9, starter weapon in the right hand at column 10–11 (staffs run the full height), shield or off-hand on the left at columns 0–1. Faces front, light from the top left (H light edge on the left, N/d shade on the right), rope belt across the waist, grey leg wraps and dark foot wraps.
- **The head carries the class:** every class gets its own head shape so it reads as a solid black silhouette (crown of leaves, headband with tails, iron cap, pot helm, pointed hood, tall hood, coif, mask, brimmed hat, cone cap, horns, topknot, feathered cap). Faces show with two eye pixels and a gap; hidden-face classes show only glowing or dark eyes.
- **One body detail per class** beyond the shared rags: torn floor-length robe (Necro), bare arms and wraps (Monk), half-cape (Shadow Knight), quiver (Ranger), tabard (Paladin), stole (Magician), bone necklace and fur mantle (Shaman), puffed sleeves and lute (Bard).
- **Walk:** two step frames, one foot lifted a pixel each; robes can hide it until the animation pass.
- **Colours** are a few swappable slots per class (cloth ramp H/n/N/d, hood A/a/Q, accent R), which is what makes recolours cheap later.

## Player attack poses (character lane, v0.112, Nov 27; owner OK'd the draft 2 bodies for all 14 classes)
- **The bodies** are the approved draft 2 (`tools/player_pass_draft.py`): a fist on every weapon, shield classes share the Warrior's frames (round plank shield held in front, bare sword arm), robe casters have a real right sleeve with the fist on the staff, the Magician stays short. `tools/player_frames.py` writes them into the game as `PLAYER15` (standing, two step frames, poses); edit the draft, then rerun it.
- **Poses are real frames, never a moved or rotated sprite:** each is a wind-up frame then a strike frame. Warrior, Paladin, Shadow Knight: **chop** for the main attack (sword raised over the shoulder, then down and forward) and **thrust** for abilities (draw back low at the hip, then lunge). Rogue stab, Ranger bow draw, Monk punch (1-px arm, 2 x 2 wrapped fist), Bard strum. Robe casters **cast** (staff lifted 3 rows, glow above the staff and in the raised off hand) and hold it for a spell's whole cast; for a melee main attack (Cleric's Smite, Shaman's Jab) they lift the staff without the glow.
- **Timing:** main attack 0.07 s wind-up + 0.16 s strike; abilities 0.09 + 0.2; instant spells 0.08 + 0.18. Looks only: nothing waits on the pose.
- **A wider pose keeps the body still:** each frame carries the column that lines up with the standing body's middle (`x`), mirrored when facing left, so only the weapon reaches out.
- **Flair (owner, Nov 27): the main attack stays plain** (it happens constantly). **Abilities get a little to start:** an area swing gets a thin pale arc sweeping round in front (radius up to 13 px); a single-target hit gets a short pale thrust streak and 3 tiny sparks on the target. **Start small, grow later:** attacks can get bigger and more dramatic as the game goes on (e.g. with level or rank), same idea as spells growing with rank. Spell looks belong to the Spells lane.
- **Heals and self moves** (Bandage, Mend) get no weapon pose; only moves that hit an enemy do.

**v0.116 additions (owner, Nov 27):**
- A melee swing never ends with the blade pointing at the ground; that reads as stabbing down. End a slash just below level, pointing at the enemy (the knights' main attack).
- Poses never stop the legs: while moving, a pose frame borrows the walk frame's legs (bottom 5 rows, standing columns 0-10; anything at column 11+, like a staff foot, stays the pose's). Standing still keeps the pose's own legs.
- Shadows sit on whole pixels, the same width either side of the centre, and under the feet (the boots' middle column, mirrored when facing left), not under the picture's middle.

## Channel glow (character lane, v0.130, Nov 27; owner asked for it)
- **Only spells with a cast time** charge it; instant spells keep the quick staff lift, physical casts (Bandage) keep their small sparkle.
- **Where:** the top of the held weapon in the cast pose (grip spot moved up by the weapon's grip row; the pose holds it upright); the open hand with no weapon; in front of the body (6 px out, 10 up) for classes with no cast pose.
- **How it's drawn:** the spell's own colour. A soft round glow added on top of the dark (radius 3 → 9 px, strength 0.3 → 0.8 as the bar fills, steady, no pulse); a bright cream core pixel that grows a 3 × 3 cross past half; and a light pool cut out of the darkness, radius 8 → 26 px.
- **No motes or flutter (owner, Nov 27, v0.133):** the caster just holds the staff up and the tip glows bright; no specks drifting in, no pulsing, and the ground light doesn't flicker.
- **Sized by mana (owner, Nov 27, v0.135):** how big and bright the glow gets comes from the spell's mana cost in three steps: small (under 10 mana) peak glow 5 px at 0.5 strength, ground light 14 px; medium (10–19) 7 px, 0.65, 20 px; big (20+) 9 px, 0.8, 26 px, and only big gets the bright cross past half. Cast-time spells grow to their step as the bar fills; **instant spells** flash straight to their step at the staff tip for the length of the cast pose (0.26 s) and fade. Knobs: `GLOW_TIER` in `js/anim.js`.
- **Size rule:** small to start. Longer casts read as more energy just by filling longer; it can grow bigger and more dramatic later (owner's flavor rule).

## Animation and effects (animation lane, v0.102)
- **Physical attacks (owner, Nov 27): small mobs attack with their bodies.** You see what physically happens: the wolf crouches, springs and bites with its **own jaws** (no fangs floating over you, not a Pokemon-style move); a skeleton **swings the sword in its hand**. Floating effects (slashes, fangs, glows) are only for spells and spell-like moves. Bigger hits and heavier attacks for bigger mobs are a later idea (backlog).
- **Mouths are red inside (owner, Nov 27, v0.127):** any open mouth shows the wolves' mouth red (`#7a1a22`) inside, for every mob. Picked over black on a side-by-side page (black read flat; brown looked bad on the Pale Rat).
- **Rat death (v0.122, `death:'flip'`):** a yelp hop (2 px up, legs spread), then belly-up: the body drawn upside down on the ground, light and dark shades swapped so the pale belly faces up, eye shut; two leg kicks (the step frame upside down), then gone at once with a 0.15 s fade. No lying there (owner).
- **Head-back poses keep the face (owner, Nov 27):** when a mob rears its head back (the Pale Rat, Old Gnawbone), draw it from its own head: same size, eye in the same place on the face; tip the snout up a pixel at a time toward the front and open the mouth in the gap. Never redraw a bigger or differently shaped face. Old Gnawbone carries his bone curled in his tail, not in his mouth.
- **How a melee attack is drawn (draft 1, Nov 27; `tools/mob_attack_draft.py`):** real frames from the live sprite, never a rotated sprite. The weapon and arm are cut out of the body and drawn per pose (arm bone from the shoulder, hand, grip, crossguard, blade, black outline). Beats: **ready → wind-up held (the tell, ~0.3 s; the King 0.45 s) → one smear frame (~0.05 s, the arc filled with the weapon's two brightest colours, no outline) → impact held (~0.13 s, 2 px lunge, a thin pale streak behind the blade; the King's blade bites the floor with dust) → recover.** Wolf bite: crouch (body down 1 px, head low) → lunge with the head redrawn jaws-open (fangs top and bottom, red mouth) → snap **fully shut**, held: teeth clenched in one row, **no red mouth showing** (owner, Nov 27) → pull back, mouth shows again. Greyfang and Old Scar use the same recipe (v0.117; one `bite()` helper takes the head column, the jaws-open head and the shut rows). **In the game since v0.114** (`MOB_POSES` in `sprites.js`, written by `python3 tools/mob_attack_draft.py --game`; `poseFrame` in `anim.js` picks the frame: wind-up while `m.atk` counts down the last moment before the hit, then smear, hit, recover after it). The wind-up plays in the moment before the hit already lands, so timing and the sim don't change.
- **Looks only.** Animations never change a timer, a number or where a mob really stands; they move the drawing. Visual randomness uses `fr()` in `js/anim.js` (Math.random), never the game's `rng()`, so the sim rolls the same dice. Nothing is spawned while the sim runs.
- **Readable crowds (owner, Nov 27): animation, not badges.** No "x4" over a stack (breaks immersion). Each mob shows its own swing: it **leans back a pixel** in the last 0.3 s before a swing, then **lunges** toward whoever it hits (3–6 px by size, out fast in about 0.06 s, back over 0.2 s) and a **pale slash** sweeps across the target. Swings stay timed from when each mob reached you (no forced staggering), so a stack ripples. Legs stop stepping while a mob stands and swings.
- **No hit reaction yet (owner, Nov 27):** a white flash on hit looked too much like dying. Hits get their look later, with weapon swings (every weapon will be animated).
- **No spark burst on kills (owner, Nov 27, v0.125):** deaths are each family's own animation; the old white spark burst on every kill is removed.
- **Death, default:** the body **sinks** (squashes toward its feet) and fades over about 0.55 s while dust puffs out; no white flash. Vanishing bosses keep their own exit.
- **Family deaths (v0.105):** a mob can have its own death (`death:` in `js/data/mobs.js`); the rest use the default. First one, **skeletons (`death:'bones'`, crypt skeletons and the Skeleton King):** the body below the skull caves in over 0.14 s while 7 bones (11 for the King) spill out and land in a **mound**, higher in the middle, lying **roughly flat** (an upright bone reads as a stick); small bones are a 1-px cream line with a shadow pixel under it and a knob at each end. The **skull is cut from the sprite itself** (`deathHead` = where it sits in the grid, so it keeps its red eyes and the King keeps his crown): it **hangs a beat** (0.24 s) while the body falls away under it, then drops onto the top of the pile, bounces once and settles. Dust puffs when the bones land and when the skull does. The pile lies about 3 s, then fades. Ideas for later families: rats flop and twitch, wolves slump onto their side, wisps snuff out in smoke, cultists drop their robes in a heap.
- **Screen shake is for story only (owner, Nov 27):** never on ordinary hits or boss attacks.
- **Glow over the dark:** effects that must read in a dark room (the Skeleton King's bone) draw **after the lighting** (`fxDrawLit`) with a soft additive glow, like flames do. Bodies and slashes draw before it so they sit in the room's light.
- **Skeleton King's Bone Throw (owner picked, Nov 27):** same rules as the old bolt (2 s cast, stun or fear breaks it, out of range it falls short). Recipe: during the cast he lifts a **12-px thigh bone** over his head (dark 4-px outline, 2-px cream shaft, two knobs each end, a shaded lower edge; the dog-bone outline is what reads), rising and trembling near the end while purple grave-light motes are pulled into it and a soft purple glow grows. He throws it **0.32 s before the cast ends** so it lands with the hit: it **arcs** (14 px high), spins, leaves two faint copies behind and bone dust, with a shadow on the floor. **Hit:** it cracks apart into cream shards and small spinning bone pieces that bounce. **Dodged:** it glances off and tumbles away. **Out of range or broken off:** it clatters to the floor and lies a moment.

- **Greyfang's Howl (v0.110; owner: v0.107's rings were "way too much"; draw the lean-back instead of turning the sprite; he keeps running, no stop-and-charge, that reuses the boar):** looks only. For about 1 s he shows his **howl frame** (`WOLF_SPR.greyfanghowl`, 5 rows taller: head thrown back, neck stretched up and forward with a spiky ruff, muzzle up and right, mouth open with fangs, ears laid back, chest sloping back under the head; `greyfanghowlstep` has his walking legs so he runs in it), with a 0.06 s beat in his normal pose either side, and **three short lines** (3 px, pale white-blue) pulse from his nose tip every 0.22 s like a Pokemon cry. **Never rotate a sprite to fake a pose; draw it as its own frame** (owner). **Don't give a named a mechanic another mob already has** (owner: the boar charges). ## UI look (owner, Nov 18–20)
Minimal EverQuest-style until the theme is decided; every screen part uses one look (UI breakdowns in `docs/research/ui.md`).
- **One panel, one button (v0.60, breakdown 19 B):** frames, HUD buttons and every window are plain grey `rgba(40,40,40,.97)` with a 1px `#8a8a8a` edge and 4px corners; buttons `#2b2b2b` with the same edge; inner cards (spells, forks, gear) `#2b2b2b` with a `#6c6c6c` edge. No gradients, no shine.
- **Gold means "on / selected"** (`#c9a227` edge, `#ffd27a` text, `#34312a` fill), plus window titles and section headings so long pages scan. The one main action (start screen Play) is grey with a gold edge.
- **Tabs:** the open tab is a lighter fill (`#4a4a4a`) with white text, never gold, so the pulsing gold "skill choice waiting" is the only gold on a tab row.
- **Effects (v0.58, breakdown 18 B):** buffs and debuffs are 14px spell icons in a grey square; a dark shade fills down as they run out, a corner number only under 10s, a red edge blinking in the last 3s.
- **Never pause (v0.56):** windows sit over a light cover with the world running; anything needing the player pulses where they go to act.
- **Maps (v0.61, breakdown 20 B, owner: "clean, simple, small"):** the minimap and full map draw the ground, never one flat square per tile: flat area colours, trees and water as rounded masses (one circle per tile, radius 0.72 of a tile, no highlights), roads as clean lines half a tile wide, buildings plain grey blocks, a soft fog edge. Pins are 5 × 5 pictures with a black outline in their kind's colour (way out green pointer, Town off-white house, way down purple arch, campfire orange flame, named red skull, chest gold). **You (v0.62.1, owner):** a slim white pointer with a thin black edge (a dart: tip, two swept-back wings, a notch at the back; length about 6 pin pixels, width about 5, edge about 1 pin pixel), drawn as a clean shape and turned to the exact heading. Tried first as a 7 × 7 pixel arrow with the pins' full outline (v0.61.2): the owner found it chunky, and pixel arrows that small go lumpy on the diagonals. Keep the marker slim and simple, never a ring, glow or gold dot. Nothing else on a map may be a white pointer. Keep map markers small: the owner prefers the small look to chunky icons; players can pick Small (default), Medium or Large pins (v0.61.1, `settings.pins`), so draw pins to read at Small.
- Don't show text for things that aren't there (e.g. a sell note with no merchant nearby).
- **Camera: you're always in the middle (v0.78, owner Nov 24, upright and sideways).** The view keeps your character centred everywhere, map edges included; it never stops at an edge. So every area has to look right past all four edges and the corners. **How it's drawn:** ground-only copies of each edge, 256 game pixels deep (`EDGE_STRIP`, taken before any scenery), are mirrored past the edge; corners mirror the top or bottom strip both ways. Outdoors, `edgeWoods` grows trees 14 tiles deep past every side: the edge's own trees carry on, roads and clearings that run off the map stay open, and further out the gaps close into forest (a few clearings stay). Caves and dungeons just show their mirrored floor going dark. The v0.55 check stays as a safety net: if a very short screen ever puts you under the top frames or the spell grid, the view nudges.

- **Enchant look (v0.100, Items lane):** an enchanted item shows **+N** before its name everywhere (worn slot, bag square, detail strip). Glow is one teal (`RAR.orb`, #7fe0d8) as a text shadow: **+1–2 none, +3 faint** (3 px, 70%), **+5 strong** (two layers, 3 px and 7 px) (`.ench3` / `.ench5` in `css/game.css`). Enchanting throws a teal spark burst from the character (16 sparks, 22 at +3, 30 at +5). Orbs and dust share the teal so they read as one family. When gear shows on the character, the same +3 / +5 steps light the sprite.

## Layout
- Main road plus small side paths that reward curiosity.
- Loops, not dead ends; a shortcut back to the hub where it fits.
- **Not every area needs two ways out (owner, Nov 20):** a cave or burrow can have one way in and out when that fits what it is (the rat tunnel: just the sinkhole). Never put two entrances to the same place right next to each other.
- Harder with distance and depth, never with locked doors.
- 1–2 secrets per area off the main road (a hidden chest, a side path, a rare spawn spot).
- Light as breadcrumbs: torches at entrances, one lit marker in view at a time.

## Levels (owner, Nov 18)
- **Plan the whole zone's levels before building the next area.** The level ladder in `design.md` (Zone 1) is the one source of truth; update it first, then build.
- **Every area is a step up at its top end.** Bands can overlap (owner, Nov 18: overlap gives a choice of where to level, EverQuest-style, as long as the areas play differently), but each area's top level is higher than the last one's.
- **Easy at the door, hard at the far end:** spread an area's band across it (low end by the entrance, top end deepest), so a wide band still feels like a climb.
- **The named is the fight for the top of its area's band:** most classes should beat it by then in the sim (the Skeleton King, level 7, is beaten at 6–9, which matches the crypt's 6–9). Its own level number can sit inside the band. The area's rare sits at the top of the band.
- **The dungeon comes when it comes.** The first dungeon can be at 12, 13 or 15; what matters is a smooth climb to it, not hitting a set level.
- **Check the pace when an area is built:** clearing an area normally (plus its named) should take you up through most of its band. Too fast and you outlevel the next area; too slow and you grind. Tune spawns, XP or the band, and note it on the board.

## Phone readability
- **Never pause (owner, Nov 20; v0.56 look, owner picked A):** windows sit over a light cover (about 38% black) so the world shows around them, and open below the top frames, which stay on top. Something that needs the player never opens a window: it pulses. **How it's drawn:** a waiting choice is a slow gold breathing outline on its button (1.2 s, grey to #ffd27a with a 2 px soft ring), never the steady small gold light the toggles use; a hit while a window is open turns the window's border red (#e24b4a, 2 px) for about a third of a second; yes/no questions are a small grey strip under the cast bar (HUD frame style, gold title, two square buttons), never a window.
- **You're never under the HUD (v0.55):** the camera keeps your character below the top bars and above the spell grid, scrolling past the map edge when it has to. Past the edge the area's ground carries on (a ground-only mirror of the edge), never black. Map edges still need their own scenery so that strip looks right (zone edges card).
- Mobs and the player must stand out from the ground: different lightness, not just a different colour.
- **Light comes from the world, not the player (owner, Nov 21; v0.72):** your character carries no light. A spot too dark to read gets a light source that belongs there (candle, brazier, lit window, lantern, a crack of daylight), placed where someone would really put it or where light would really fall, never a glow on the player. **How it's drawn:** lights are entries in the area's `lights` (radius `r`, warm amount `w`, cold amount `c`) cut out of the area's ambient darkness (`AREA_TINT`); keep the ambient high enough that you and mobs still read between lights. `PLAYER_LIGHT` in `js/render.js` (radius 95) is the off switch, kept only for testing.
- Effects (fog, rain, ash) never hide a mob or the target ring for long.

### Mob cast poses (v0.147, owner OK'd draft 1, Nov 27)
- **Every caster holds a pose for the whole cast** and the light builds as the bar fills, in **four steps** (a spark, a small cross, a 5-pixel glow, a 6-pixel glow with a white-hot core); motes drift in from step 1. Then **one release frame** when it goes off, then back to normal. Interrupted casts just stop (no release).
- **Spell colours** are digits 1–4 in the frame palette (dark rim, the spell's own colour from `mobs.js`, bright, white core), so they never clash with a sprite's letters: Ash Bolt orange, Shadow Bolt purple, Cinder Lash amber, Mend green, Rite of Mending gold, Grasp of the Deep sea-green, the chant lilac.
- **Bolts are lifted, then thrown:** the hand (or staff, or censer) goes up over the head where the glow gathers; the release is the arm thrust at you with a short star of light. **Heals** gather between raised hands (mender) or light up the caster's own trim (Malrec), and release as a ring or a flash along the floor.
- **Instant moves get a short drawn move instead of a hold:** the chanter sways his censer twice with motes rising, the great ash wisp swells and pulses while its ring burns, the Acolyte tips his censer and embers spill for Ember Brand.
- **Physical moves stay physical:** the Bellringer's Toll is a man heaving an iron clapper (it rises a step each beat and shakes with the strain: the tell), then slamming it down. **Iron stays straight and upright (owner, Nov 27, twice):** the clapper never tilts or bends on the slam (a tilted bar read as a bent spoon). He drives it **straight down in front of him**: the same clapper drawn head-down (flipped top to bottom, never turned), his fist on top of the handle, the iron head on the floor. Awkward is fine; bent is not.
- How it's drawn: `tools/cast_poses_draft.py` (draft page with `tools/cast_review_page.py`), `--game` writes `MOB_CASTS` into `js/data/sprites.js`; `castFrame` / `castPlay` in `js/anim.js` pick the frame, the cast code in `js/world.js` calls `castPlay` when a spell goes off.

## Gear looks by zone (character lane, v0.142, Nov 27; owner decided)
- **One look step per zone.** Each step of the look ladder (`tools/look_ladder_draft.py 2`, draft 2 is the order) belongs to a zone, not to a rarity: an item shows its zone's look, so Zone 1 gear reads as Zone 1. Small details climb zone by zone (a hat bobble, a belt, trim, a collar; studs, mail, a tabard, a plume); capes, crests and glow wait for far-later zones. Later, a harder dungeon tier of an item can upgrade its look.
- **Showing slots:** chest and head in Zone 1 (weapons already show, v0.121). Hands, legs, feet don't show; back (capes) only late.
- **Zone 1 is colour only, but a real upgrade (owner, Nov 27, v0.145):** v0.142's small step read as "pretty much nothing". Zone 1 gear turns washed-out rags into **the class's own colour, clearly brighter and easier to read** (Warrior bright red, not leather: keep the class colour, never swap it for a duller material), plus **a gold or class-colour trim on the belt or sash** (`y`). The chest item swaps the clothes ramp (H n N d, the belt, plus the Cleric's white and gold, the Magician's red stripes, the Monk's sash); the head item brightens the hat, hood or helm. No new pixels.
- **How it's drawn:** `js/data/looks.js` (`GEAR_LOOKS[class][slot][zone]` = letter → colour, from `tools/gear_looks.py`). `dress()` in `js/render.js` swaps the worn slots' letters for stand-in letters (1–5 head, 6–9 @ # chest) before the picture is made; head swaps apply only above the shoulders (rows before the first row with H or n), chest swaps only from there down, so a letter both parts use changes in the right place only. Every frame (standing, both steps, every pose) is dressed, and the picture is cached per weapon + chest look + head look.
- **A later zone that changes shape** (new pixels: a bobble, a collar, a plume) needs per-frame pixel overlays anchored to the head and body; the head moves in some poses (`HEADBOX` in `tools/player_pass_draft.py`), so overlays follow it.

## Weapon looks (owner, Nov 27)
- **Looks follow power, never rarity colour.** A stronger weapon never looks plainer than a weaker one. Zone 1 uses looks 1–4. **Size stops growing at look 4**: from Zone 2 on, weapons keep that size and step up in metal (e.g. Zone 2 blue-black steel with a rune line), trim, gems and finally glow, so characters stay small and detailed.
- **How it's drawn** (`tools/weapon_looks.py`, held in the right hand at 15×18): each step up makes the weapon longer, wider or fancier, never just busier. Look 1 plain dull iron and wood; 2 steel, a little longer; 3 bright steel with brass, a 2-wide blade or bigger head; 4 polished with gold and **one** gem pixel, widest shape. Power reads from proportion at this size: widen the blade and lengthen the guard rather than adding detail (references: pixel weapon tier packs, Nov 27).
- **Named weapons** keep their tier's shape and swap colours to their maker: Skeleton King bone grip and trim with a violet gem; Malrec soot-dark iron with an ember edge and gold-orange trim.
- **No weapon = empty hand**, so taking a weapon off is easy to see.
- **One type per class** (list in `design.md`). Shared types tell classes apart with a gem in the class colour from look 2; maces add a class-colour handle (Cleric pale gold, Paladin blue); the Shadow Knight's sword has a black hilt and dark iron guard. **Crook** (Druid): living wood with a curled hook at the top and leaves, no point. **Scepter** (Magician, Enchanter): half a staff's length with an orb head in the class colour, so it never reads as a staff. Monks carry nothing.

The weapon's upright drawing is turned to that angle about its grip and drawn **behind** the body, so the fist covers the grip.
- Weapon drawings are upright, at game size, with a grip row (`tools/weapon_looks.py`). The look follows power, never rarity colour; size stops growing at look 4; named keep their tier's shape in their maker's colours.

- A new pose frame needs nothing extra: draw it holding the old weapon and `tools/player_frames.py` cuts it out and measures the grip and angle (rules per class in `weapon_px`).
